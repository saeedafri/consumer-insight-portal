# Anonymous final council responses

Proposal and evidence: ../../02-final-review-question.md. Evaluate content without advisor identities.

## Response A

**Verdict:** The proposal is executable as a discovery-and-reliability program; its differentiation is still unproven. A buyer purchases an answer that changes a decision, not pinned versions or a better survey browser.

**Observed:** CIP offers retail survey exploration and internal frozen publications; its current React workflow has delivery and consistency gaps. The evidence packet reports public vendor capabilities, not tenant-tested results. Circana/NIQ/Numerator address purchase measurement, while GWI/YouGov address audience intelligence; Displayr/Forsta can improve reporting over supplied data. These are different purchases. The cheapest credible alternative may be an existing analyst emailing a spreadsheet and two slides.

**Change:** Specify the purchased deliverable before naming the workflow: for example, a category manager’s recurring, shareable evidence brief answering one agreed retail decision, with the supported population, source dates, uncertainty, material-change rule and permitted recipients. This example is a proposal, not established demand. Identify who commissions it, who reads it, who approves expenditure and whether a decision can change on the tracker’s cadence. Test a staff-produced brief first, with preparation effort counted; only automate steps that repeat. A customer need not adopt a portal to value the product.

**Reject:** Treating faster chart production as sufficient differentiation. Buyers already paying for a subscription require incremental evidence or a meaningful reduction in their entire decision process. Compare actual alternatives separately; incompatible datasets cannot support a speed-based superiority claim.

**Most consequential blind spot:** Distribution and acceptance. A defensible brief still fails commercially if recipients cannot reuse it in their established decision meeting, licensing prevents forwarding, or the budget owner sees duplication.

**First action:** During authorized discovery, reconstruct one buyer’s last real decision using its existing brief, approval path and purchased sources; agree the next deliverable and acceptance/payment conditions before designing its workspace.

## Response B

Proceed conditionally. The revised sequence is executable, but its gates need decision owners and bounded outcomes before funding. Twenty-three public competitor families establish alternatives and workflow patterns; they do not establish comparative performance or a differentiated product advantage.

**Observed:** Trends failed and Compare returned 404 in the running application while newer source contains the route. Checkout tests therefore cannot certify the customer release. The cause remains unconfirmed. **Proposed:** assign one release owner to capture frontend/API/data identities and one failing request chain, distinguish route/proxy/version faults, then replay the same journey on the matched candidate. Require explicit pass evidence for access parity, production sessions and applicable numerical/export contracts before external pilot access.

Change the first slice from an open-ended “saved analysis/brief” into one buyer-selected decision, named persona, supported question/audience/wave set, refresh policy and export format. Require a method owner to certify that this exact slice supports repeated use. Preserve frozen outputs when refreshed data become incomparable; expose the reason instead of manufacturing continuity.

The consequential blind spot is an unbounded trust-remediation programme consuming capacity before evidence of purchase. Classify gates into universal access obligations, slice-specific analytical correctness and deferred capabilities; never waive an access path because the interface hides it.

Before pilot work, the commercial and engineering owners must sign a scorecard with a capacity ceiling, review date, minimum repeat-use/payment signal and maximum total delivery effort. Stop expansion when those pre-agreed limits fail; quarantine a numerically invalid or unauthorized output immediately. Numerical thresholds require owner approval, not invention here.

**First action:** convene the release owner and buyer sponsor to select one real recurring decision and attach its reproducible failing/current journey to that scorecard.

## Response C

The proposal is executable, but risks making a defensible report its destination. Public evidence already places reusable reporting with Displayr/Forsta and audience workspaces with GWI/MRI-Simmons. Trust reduces purchase risk; it does not establish a distinctive reason to buy.

**Observed:** CIP has rotating retail questions, harmonised concepts, backend cohorts and frozen publications. The packet does not establish the repeat-question coverage, suitable bases or commercial rights needed for any selected recurring decision. Competitor capabilities are advertised public claims, not tenant-tested results.

**Change:** Test a decision-to-evidence loop alongside the first brief. A buyer records the decision, evidence needed, deadline and uncertainty. CIP shows which questions can answer it, which comparisons are unsupported and which evidence is missing. After the decision, capture whether evidence changed the action and what follow-up would resolve the remaining uncertainty. This creates reusable retail decision templates rather than another report builder.

Do not defer every follow-up research capability behind payment for the initial brief: the existing tracker may fail precisely because it never asks the buyer's necessary question. Prototype a governed research-request intake and manually cost a supplier/service response during discovery. This needs no new panel, transaction licence or broad survey-authoring platform. Separate exploratory demand evidence from authorization to field research.

**Consequential blind spot:** The recommendation assumes a valuable decision is already answerable from existing data. A failed pilot could otherwise reject the workflow when the true constraint is questionnaire coverage.

**First action:** For one buyer decision, create a question-by-decision coverage map with research staff and the buyer: available, usable, insufficient base, incompatible or missing. Compare an existing-data brief with a priced follow-up option; record preference, willingness to pay and total delivery cost before selecting the slice.

## Response D

The revised proposal is executable as a validation sequence, but differentiation remains unproven. A sourced brief is valuable only if its evidence changes a decision that the buyer cannot resolve more cheaply elsewhere. Provenance and reliable exports are necessary quality conditions; public competitor claims already include reporting and evidence workflows.

**Observed:** the latest displayed tracker wave has n=400 overall; recurring question coverage, usable audience bases and unique-person counts are not independently established. CIP has harmonisation and frozen publication foundations, but no demonstrated transaction network. **Inference:** a retail decision requiring narrow category buyers or small movements may exceed the available data even after software defects are corrected. Independent weekly samples of this size can support some descriptive work while remaining inadequate for the proposed decision threshold; actual adequacy depends on design, incidence and overlap.

**Change:** replace “weekly retail brief” as the initial product definition with a buyer-approved decision contract: decision owner, action options, deadline, minimum decision-relevant difference, required audience, available alternatives and consequences of being wrong. Require a research lead to mark each necessary question/time window supported, insufficient or unavailable before building its workspace. Pooling waves requires an explicit time estimand and stable instruments; more observations cannot silently repair incompatible questions. Add an outcome measure: did the brief change, confirm or accelerate an accountable action? Keep correctness and preparation effort as separate measures.

**Most consequential blind spot:** the commercial workflow may require evidence CIP cannot supply, making a polished brief an efficient delivery mechanism for an inconclusive answer.

**First action:** reconstruct one recent buyer decision using its actual brief and CIP's question/base/wave inventory. Produce a signed buyer/research feasibility assessment before funding the saved-analysis slice; conduct release/access corrections concurrently.

## Response E

The proposal is executable as a gated experiment, but still mistakes a defensible delivery workflow for differentiated customer value. Provenance and saved briefs are necessary capabilities; the public competitor evidence already advertises analogous reporting and research workflows. Engineering success would establish competence, not a reason to switch or add another subscription.

**Observed:** CIP has rotating retail questions, harmonisation and frozen publications; the latest displayed wave has n=400. The audit does not establish persistent coverage or sufficient audience bases for a particular recurring commercial decision. Circana, NIQ and Numerator offer different purchase evidence; their advertised capabilities do not prove tenant performance. CIP cannot overcome an evidence mismatch through better packaging.

**Change:** Require a named decision, accountable buyer, current paid alternative and a consequential unanswered question before committing to the saved-analysis product slice. Test whether existing Coresight research can answer that question more usefully than the buyer's current bundle. If it cannot, reject the proposed wedge even if users like its exports. Avoid selecting only tasks that CIP's questionnaire happens to support.

Run a manually assembled, permission-cleared brief first. Compare it with the current Coresight service as well as external tools: automation may cannibalize profitable analyst work without creating incremental demand. Record displaced revenue and analyst opportunity cost alongside operating costs. Preserve the release/access gates before exposing restricted application data, but do not make the entire remediation backlog a prerequisite for commercial discovery.

**Single blind spot:** the recommendation lacks evidence that the underlying information changes a funded decision enough to justify incremental spend.

**First action:** interview one budget owner using their last real retail decision and existing deliverable; obtain an explicit purchase or replacement criterion before choosing the pilot workflow.
