# Anonymous peer review 1

**Strongest: D.** It converts an attractive brief into a falsifiable decision contract and connects the displayed sample limitation to audience incidence, instrument continuity and the decision-relevant difference. Requiring buyer/research feasibility before funding the workspace prevents software improvements from masking an evidence deficit. Its sampling conclusions remain appropriately conditional.

**Biggest blind spot: C.** Research-request intake could reveal missing demand, but adding a priced supplier response risks turning a portal validation into a bespoke research-service experiment. A buyer preferring custom research does not validate repeatable portal economics. Require separate demand, delivery-cost and renewal criteria for existing-data briefs and commissioned research before interpreting either as product validation.

**Shared omission:** All rely heavily on one reconstructed buyer decision and reported usefulness. Friendly-buyer selection, hindsight and staff assistance can make a weak proposition appear commercially credible. One decision is suitable for discovery, not an investment conclusion. Predefine a small set of prospect types, include a dissatisfied/non-converting prospect, and test prospective briefs using acceptance criteria set before results are known. Record refusals, staff interventions and unusable evidence. Separately assess willingness to purchase the information and willingness to purchase portal automation; otherwise a successful concierge service can falsely justify workspace investment.
