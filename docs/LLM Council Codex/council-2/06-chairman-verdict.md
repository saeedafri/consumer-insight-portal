# Final Council verdict

Chairman: coordinating Codex agent. Inputs: five independent final advisor responses, five anonymous peer reviews, the updated recommendation, and the REA product/market packet. This is a multi-perspective review, not five independent model providers or an empirical customer study.

## Where the Council Agrees

Proceed with a bounded validation program. Reliable access, compatible releases and correct supported results are prerequisites for external release. They do not establish differentiation.

Identify a purchased decision deliverable before building a workspace. Research must verify whether existing questions, audience bases, fieldwork and repeated instruments can support that decision. A faster report containing inconclusive evidence is not a competitive win.

Use a permission-cleared manual brief to learn which steps repeat, then automate those steps. Keep the current architecture and existing analytical assets. Require buyer, method and delivery owners, cost limits and stopping rules. Broad AI, transaction data and arbitrary segmentation remain contingent investments.

## Where the Council Clashes

The Expansionist wants a research-request intake and priced follow-up option during discovery, because the tracker may omit the buyer's essential question. Four reviewers identify this as the largest scope risk: demand for custom research can masquerade as demand for a reusable portal.

Resolve this by recording unmet questions in the decision coverage map. A manual supplier feasibility/price investigation can be a separate capped experiment if the commercial and research owners choose it. Do not bundle its success into portal validation, field a survey without scope/rights approval, or promise turnaround.

The Executor favors a release-and-buyer scorecard as the first move. The First Principles, Contrarian and Outsider emphasize a real budget-owner decision. Start the scorecard from that real decision; attach the current technical journey. Access correction remains mandatory independently of sales validation.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught

A one-buyer retrospective replay is discovery evidence. It cannot establish market demand, competitive superiority or self-service adoption. Recruit multiple target prospect types, include non-converting or dissatisfied prospects, and test a prospective task with acceptance criteria set before results are known. Record refusals, staff coaching and unsuccessful cases.

Larger bases, stable wording, quotas and weights cannot establish population validity by themselves. Inspect recruitment, coverage/nonresponse, quality exclusions and measurement validity; compare with an appropriate independent benchmark where available. If validation is unavailable, constrain claims to the observed respondent population. Pooling needs a defined time population and stable instrument.

Rights must cover source ingestion, derived aggregates, AI use, exports, recipient reuse and retention. “Shareable” is a product promise requiring a data contract.

Commercial alternatives may already be available under an enterprise subscription at zero marginal user cost. Compare incremental budget value and Coresight service cannibalization, not only preparation time. Include exception-heavy weeks, analyst opportunity cost, supplier minimums and customer obligations after stopping.

Peer preference: First Principles3, Executor1, Contrarian1. Biggest-blind-spot selections: Expansionist4, Executor1. These are five qualitative reviews, not validated probabilities.

## The Recommendation

**Build a trusted decision-to-evidence workflow, and earn the right to expand it.** Four explicit decisions govern investment:

1. **Can CIP lawfully expose the evidence?** Uniform access policy and production API identity must pass, including titles/search/static files. A source/runtime contract mismatch must be resolved on an identified release.
2. **Can the data answer the decision?** The research owner signs a question/base/wave feasibility map and claim boundary. If evidence is missing, record a research or partner need; do not fund UI as its substitute.
3. **Will a buyer fund the deliverable over available alternatives?** Define buyer, user, recipient, action, deadline, existing spend and concrete procurement/payment criterion. Test a manual brief and prospective repetition before choosing automation.
4. **Can delivery remain repeatable and economic?** Own the research specification and provenance; evaluate commodity rendering. Automate one saved brief and one chosen format. Keep customer/staff time separate and test without coaching. Commercial and method owners have explicit stop authority.

Only after these decisions pass should product select the next constraint: audience reuse, alerting, integrations or cited descriptive AI. Purchase/POS/receipt data and predictive/causal products need separate rights, scientific and commercial gates.

The final recommendation is **conditional approval of a focused product experiment**, not approval of the entire feature backlog and not a claim that CIP already beats competitors.

## The One Thing to Do First

Create one buyer-approved decision contract from a recent real retail decision, then have the research owner map each required fact to a supported CIP question, population, wave and usable base. Mark each supported, insufficient, incompatible or missing. This document selects the first slice and reveals whether a product, research or data-partnership investment is warranted.
