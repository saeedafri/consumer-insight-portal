# Outsider — independent buyer perspective

As a retail analyst, I would buy a faster way to defend a recommendation in Monday’s meeting. “Harmonisation,” “own-question bases” and “FDR” describe machinery. Show me what changed, who answered, whether the comparison is valid, and a slide my manager can check. The reported broken Trends and Compare journeys undermine that promise before missing AI or purchase data matters.

Prioritize A as a release prerequisite, while validating the first useful piece of B through an analyst-assisted weekly brief. Avoid making every proposed governance mechanism a dependency for learning whether buyers value the workflow. Restrict external access until authorization and release identity are proven; use approved aggregate evidence for buyer testing.

The hidden assumption is that weekly US retail insight is a sufficiently valuable wedge. Some buyers need “why shoppers say this”; others need “which products sold.” CIP can support the former within its questionnaire coverage. Circana, NIQ and Numerator address purchase measurement that a better interface cannot replace. Treat them as possible complements. GWI, YouGov and Statista are more relevant alternatives for finding and presenting audience evidence, but advertised capabilities still need task-level validation.

Build saved questions, reusable audience definitions and one reproducible export around demonstrated buyer demand. Partner for a specific missing evidence source only when it changes a paid decision. Defer a broad chatbot and predictive claims. Measure correct brief completion, manual repair, repeat use and willingness to pay; raw feature counts and headline respondent totals will not establish value.

**First move:** ask five target buyers to bring a recent decision brief and recreate its hardest evidence task using current approved CIP outputs. Record which unanswered questions require better workflow, better questions, or genuinely different data before selecting the first workspace template.
