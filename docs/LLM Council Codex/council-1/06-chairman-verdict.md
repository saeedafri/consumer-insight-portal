# Council one verdict

Chairman: coordinating Codex agent, after reading five independent advisor responses and five anonymous peer reviews. Roles were assigned to separate agent contexts; they were not five different model providers.

## Where the Council Agrees

Protect access and make the supported research deliverable correct before restricted external release. Validate customer demand in parallel using approved evidence. A secure, statistically consistent portal is necessary; it is not proof that buyers will pay.

Build on the implemented portal rather than commissioning another broad rewrite. The best initial candidate is one saved, reproducible retail decision brief in the customer's preferred format. Existing publications/cohorts are reusable foundations subject to their versioning limitations.

Do not pursue feature-count parity across23 unlike competitors. GWI/YouGov/Statista benchmark audience research workflows; Circana/NIQ/Numerator may supply different purchase evidence. A narrow n400 wave cannot support arbitrary fine segments. Unweighted outputs can remain valid for disclosed descriptive tasks without national-representativeness promises.

## Where the Council Clashes

The Executor makes matched-release reproduction the first action; the buyer-oriented advisors make a recent decision-brief replay the first action. These resolve into parallel tracks with different owners: engineering diagnoses the release and prevents unauthorized exposure, while product/research uses permitted aggregate outputs to test the buyer task. Research cannot wait for an entire platform rebuild, and a pilot cannot become a workaround for unsafe access.

The Expansionist advocates early assistant prototypes, follow-up research and partner qualification. All five reviewers judged this the largest scope risk. Those opportunities remain hypotheses in a small option backlog; only the one shown to block a paying decision earns an experiment. A research owner can assess feasibility without opening three implementation programs.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught

Peer review identified a research supply-chain gate missing from the original plan: population/sample sourcing, quota/coverage, repeat respondents, instrument continuity, routing, recurring question coverage, fielding cadence and usable bases. Software correctness cannot supply missing evidence.

Reviewers also separated a successful analyst service from a scalable product. A supported pilot must record customer time and Coresight staff time separately, plus support, fieldwork, licences and maintenance. Repeat use or positive interviews can coexist with negative margins.

A competitive claim requires matched task evidence, not marketing comparison. Record data non-comparability separately from usability; test spreadsheets and existing analyst services as substitutes. Identify economic buyer, user and approver, existing spend and an actual procurement step.

Peer voting favored the Contrarian and Executor twice each, Outsider once. First Principles was not selected strongest; this is a five-review preference distribution, not a statistical confidence score. Unanimous criticism of Expansionist scope is a prioritization signal, not proof its upside is false.

## The Recommendation

Approve a **bounded validation and release program**, not the whole preliminary six-band roadmap.

1. **Gate protected access and release identity.** Reproduce source/runtime failures on a coordinated release; make resource authorization equivalent across API/static/catalog/search/caches; verify the production API identity/session path.
2. **Define one buyer decision and evidence boundary.** Record user, payer, geography, population, frequency, required questions, dates, usable audience bases, desired effect sensitivity and acceptable uncertainty. Research signs off the specific supported result.
3. **Deliver one reusable brief.** Save a constrained analysis specification, pin source/method/audience versions, show complete bases/provenance, and provide one buyer-selected export. Fix the correctness/publication dependencies needed for it. Suppress or disable unsupported inference rather than expanding analytical claims.
4. **Test value and economics against the real substitute.** Use equivalent available evidence. Predefine accuracy, completion-time, correction, recurrence, payment/procurement and fully loaded cost criteria. No threshold or calendar is promised without buyer/owner agreement.
5. **Expand only after the next constraint is observed.** Audience tools, alerts, AI, customer APIs, partner data and custom research each need a demand, data-adequacy, rights and operating-cost gate.

## The One Thing to Do First

Appoint a product/research owner to replay one real buyer's recent decision brief from existing approved evidence, recording its required population, source coverage, manual steps and current substitute. Engineering independently blocks wider release until G01–G03 are resolved. The replay determines what the first product slice must do; it does not authorize broader data access.
